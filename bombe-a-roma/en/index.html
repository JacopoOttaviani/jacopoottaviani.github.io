    What's on the map

    Bombe carta (paper bombs: explosive powder packed in paper and tape), large firecrackers and homemade explosive devices set off against a target in Rome, Ostia and Acilia included, from 1 January 2023 to 24 November 2026.

    The two dead counted at the top are the anarchists of the Parco degli Acquedotti (March 2026), killed, according to investigators, by the device they were assembling. At La Rustica (July 2023) the target is not known.

    The map only covers episodes reported in the press. Those that never made it into an article are missing.

    To report an incident to add to the map, or to help improve the information on this site, get in touch at jacopoottaviani.com.

    The author

    Jacopo Ottaviani is an Italian data journalist and computer scientist. His work has appeared in The Guardian, Der Spiegel and El País, among others. He writes about AI and journalism for the Reuters Institute.

    This map is one of his data journalism projects: episodes taken from the news, checked against the articles, mapped and published as open data.

    How they were located

    Each address comes from the article and was geocoded with OpenStreetMap.

    The card says how precise the point is: at the street number, at the junction, at the place named, on the street when the number is missing from the article or from OpenStreetMap (common in Rome), estimated between nearby street numbers on the Casilina, or in the area when no street is given. For private homes the point stays on the street, without a number.

    Motives and leads are those reported by the sources, attributed as investigators' hypotheses.

    Sources and data

    Outlets cited in the cards: AGI, Agenzia Nova, Dissapore, Domani, Il Fatto Quotidiano, Il Post, Internazionale, LaPresse, Open, QN Quotidiano.net, RomaToday. Each card gives the headline, date and link of every article used. The articles are in Italian; the summaries on this page are translations.

    The episodes were found through RomaToday's topic pages (“bombe”, “esplosioni”, “intimidazioni”, “attentati”) and the articles linked from them, then checked against the other outlets and against web searches by period and neighbourhood.
